## Clear ownership during incidents at Varnel

**Alerts without an owner**

Varnel had grown into several monitoring tools, each with its own alert channel. When something failed, alerts landed in shared rooms and engineers had to work out who owned the service. On a bad night, that took longer than the fix itself.

**Schedules and escalation per team**

The site reliability team moved all monitors into Navy and set up an on-call schedule for each of its 40 service teams. Every monitor belongs to a team, and every team has an escalation policy. Critical alerts go to the primary engineer by SMS and PagerDuty, then escalate to the secondary and the team lead if nobody acknowledges.

**One timeline for everyone**

Navy creates an incident timeline automatically, with response time charts and every alert and acknowledgement in order. After each incident, the owning team writes a postmortem in Navy, and action items are tracked to completion in the weekly review.

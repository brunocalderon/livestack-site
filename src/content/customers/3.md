## Fewer pages, faster response at Tessly

**Too many alerts to trust**

Tessly runs its platform in many cloud regions, and each region exposes its own APIs and endpoints. The previous monitoring setup checked them from one location. When that location had a network issue, dozens of healthy services paged at once. On-call engineers learned to wait a few minutes before acting, which slowed down response to real incidents.

**Confirmation from three regions**

With Navy, every endpoint is checked every 30 seconds from 18 regions. An outage is only confirmed when 3 regions see the same failure. In the first month, false alarm pages dropped by 92%, and engineers went back to treating every page as real.

**Escalation that matches ownership**

Each service team owns an escalation policy in Navy. Alerts go to the team's Slack channel first, then to the primary on-call engineer through PagerDuty, then to the secondary after five minutes. Response time charts and the automatic incident timeline give everyone the same picture during an incident.

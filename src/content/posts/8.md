Average response time is the most common number on a monitoring dashboard and one of the least useful. A few very slow requests disappear inside an average, but those are the requests users remember. Percentiles show what the average hides.

## 1. What a percentile means

The p95 response time is the time under which 95% of checks completed. If your p95 is 400 ms, 1 in 20 checks took longer than that. Percentiles describe the experience of real slices of traffic, not an imaginary typical request.

## 2. Read p50 as the typical case

The p50, or median, is what most requests feel like. It is a better typical value than the average because a handful of slow outliers cannot pull it up. If p50 rises, almost everyone is slower.

## 3. Read p95 and p99 as the tail

The tail is where problems start. A rising p99 with a flat p50 often points to a slow database query, a cold cache or one unhealthy instance. Navy response time charts show p50, p95 and p99 together so you can see the gap.

## 4. Compare regions

Response times from 18 regions tell you more than one number. A spike in one region usually means a network or CDN issue. A spike everywhere usually means your service. Split the chart by region before you start debugging.

## 5. Alert on percentiles, not averages

Set performance alerts on p95 or p99 over a short window. An alert on the average fires late, after the tail has already been bad for a while. Keep thresholds a little above your normal p95 so they catch real changes and ignore noise.

## 6. Watch trends over weeks

Slow regressions do not trigger alerts. Compare this week's p95 with last month's. A service that gets 10 ms slower with every release will be noticeably slower in a quarter, and nobody will know which change did it.

| Percentile | What it tells you | Typical use |
|-----------------|------|-----------------|
| p50 | The typical request | Baseline and trends |
| p95 | Most of your slow requests | Performance alerts |
| p99 | The worst regular experience | Finding tail problems |

Averages answer the wrong question. Look at p50 for the typical case, p95 and p99 for the tail, and alert on the numbers your users actually feel.
